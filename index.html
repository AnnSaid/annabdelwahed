Protect the few: retail customer retention

408 high-value customers have gone quiet, taking £1.35M of past spend with them.

Read the case studyOpen the dashboard

Business question

Who drives our revenue, who are we losing, and where should the retention budget go?

Key findings

  • The top 10% of customers bring 63.9% of all revenue.
  • 408 at-risk customers spent £1.35M, about £3,300 each, and had not ordered in almost a year.
  • Only 20.2% of new customers buy again the next month.

How I built it

  • Cleaned 1,067,371 raw rows in SQL (DuckDB) down to 5,852 customers and 36,594 orders.
  • Segmented every customer with RFM scores built from window functions.
  • Built a Tableau dashboard that ends in a ranked win-back list of who to call first.

Recommendation

Start with the win-back list: the top 10 customers alone represent about £396K. Protect the Champions, who bring 70% of revenue, and follow up with new customers within 30 days of their first order.

Tools and data

SQL (DuckDB), DBeaver, Tableau. Online Retail II, UCI Machine Learning Repository, Dec 2009 to Dec 2011.

Train the right people, not more hours

The company trains four people to find one candidate, and training hours make no difference.

Read the case studyOpen the dashboard

Business question

Which trainees are most likely to join the company, and where should the training budget go?

Key findings

  • Only 24.9% of 19,158 trainees want a new job.
  • Join rate barely moves with training hours, so more hours do not create more interest.
  • Trainees who left company details blank join at 40.6%, against 17.9% for those who filled them in. They are about half of all interested trainees.

How I built it

  • Cleaned and explored 19,158 records in SQL (DuckDB) before opening Tableau.
  • Removed gender on purpose, because a recruiting tool should never lean on it.
  • Kept blank company details after checking that the blank itself carried meaning.
  • Built a Tableau decision tool where a manager checks any trainee profile in one click.

Recommendation

Spend training and recruiting budget on the profiles that are likely to join, not on more training hours.

Tools and data

SQL (DuckDB), DBeaver, Tableau. HR Analytics: Job Change of Data Scientists (Kaggle).

Olist: how delivery delays shape customer ratings

A delay of 1 to 3 days cuts the average rating from 4.2 to 3.2, and Rio de Janeiro is the biggest problem.

Read the case studyOpen the dashboard

Business question

How do late deliveries affect customer ratings, and where should the business act first?

Key findings

  • On-time orders average 4.2 stars. After a delay of 1 to 3 days the rating falls to 3.2.
  • The late rate stayed at 3% to 5% until October 2017, then reached about 19% in March 2018.
  • Rio de Janeiro runs 12.1% late, nearly three times São Paulo, and delivers in 15.2 days against 8.7.

How I built it

  • Cleaned and joined 6 tables in SQL (DuckDB), summarizing each to one row per order first so revenue was not inflated.
  • Checked that revenue matched across two tables (R$13,179,778).
  • Built a Tableau dashboard where clicking a state filters every chart.

Recommendation

Audit last-mile delivery in Rio first, add carrier capacity before peak seasons, and act in the first days of a delay, since ratings fall within a week.

Tools and data

SQL (DuckDB), DBeaver, Tableau. Olist Brazilian E-Commerce dataset (Kaggle), 96,203 delivered orders, Jan 2017 to Aug 2018.

Food delivery: which cuisine deserves expansion?

Indian rates 4.54 against a 4.34 average with only 73 orders so far, so it is the cuisine to expand.

Read the case study

Business question

A delivery app has 14 cuisines. Which one deserves expansion, and is volume the right reason to invest?

Key findings

  • American, Japanese, Italian and Chinese bring about 82% of revenue, but their ratings are only average.
  • Indian has a 4.54 rating from 50 rated orders and a higher order value. Mexican is slightly above average.
  • Thai and Spanish have the best ratings, but they rest on 9 and 6 rated orders, which is too few to act on.

How I built it

  • Fixed the rating column in Power Query, where 736 orders held the text Not given, leaving 1,162 rated orders.
  • Added a Rated orders column and a log scale chart so small cuisines stay readable.
  • Built a one-page Power BI Service dashboard with KPIs, a cuisine table, a weekday or weekend filter and a written recommendation.

Recommendation

Expand Indian first, test Mexican before scaling, maintain the four large cuisines and watch Thai and Spanish. A short pilot is a better next step than a full rollout.

Tools and data

Power BI Service, Power Query. Food Ordering and Delivery App dataset (Kaggle), 1,898 orders.

E-commerce project tracker

Actual progress is 56.1% against 80.3% planned, so the project is 24.3 points behind.

Business question

Is the store build on schedule, and which tasks need attention?

Key findings

  • Actual progress, weighted by task duration, is 56.1% against 80.3% planned.
  • 2 of 7 tasks are complete, 2 are late and 1 is at risk of delay.

How I built it

  • Designed the dashboard in Arabic, right to left, with KPI cards for progress and variance.
  • Built a task Gantt chart with a today line, where darker bars show actual progress.
  • Added filters for status, owner and date range.

Tools and data

Power BI. A project plan with 7 tasks.
